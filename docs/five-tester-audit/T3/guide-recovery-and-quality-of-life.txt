  - heading "There is room for what is hard" [level=2]
  - generic: Recovery can touch energy, pain, sleep, concentration, confidence, mood, relationships, work and social life. Some changes are easy for other people to see; others are private or come and go. You do not have to prove that a concern is serious before raising it with a clinician.
  - heading "A metaphor some people find helpful" [level=2]
  - generic: Some people use spoon theory as a way to describe how everyday tasks can draw on a limited amount of energy, and why choices may be needed when fatigue is present. The metaphor comes from Christine Miserandino’s account of her own experience living with lupus; it is not a clinical measure, does not establish what fatigue is like after a dissection or stroke, and may not fit everyone.

  - heading "A small and limited research signal" [level=2]
  - generic: A long-term study of cervical artery dissection survivors reported that quality of life may not be captured fully by standard disability ratings. A newer exploratory study followed a selected group with good early functional outcomes and still found long-term fatigue and psychological symptoms in some participants. These studies cannot predict an individual’s experience, establish a general prevalence, or show that this app changes outcomes.
