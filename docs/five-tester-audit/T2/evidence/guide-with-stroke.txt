Recovery can involve many parts of life
Stroke effects and rehabilitation needs vary. Communication, movement, vision, thinking, mood, energy, confidence and daily activities may be part of a person’s experience. Not every effect is visible to other people, and no list can predict one person’s recovery.
You and your carer may need different information
People with stroke and the people supporting them may have different questions. Information needs can change over time, and it may help to revisit a topic with the care team instead of trying to absorb everything at once. The research behind this idea concerns stroke broadly, not dissection specifically.
Questions for the care team
•
Who coordinates my rehabilitation and follow-up?
•
How can we make appointments work for my communication, reading, memory or energy needs?
•
What support is available for me and for the person helping me?
•
Who can help with practical needs after discharge?
•
What is the best way to ask a non-emergency question between appointments?
•
Could we write down the next steps and who is responsible for each one?
If someone comes with you
You can decide whether another person joins and what you want them to do. They might listen, help you remember questions or take notes if you ask. The clinician should know how you prefer to communicate and who you want involved.
Urgent changes
If there are sudden symptoms that may be a stroke, contact local emergency services now. Do not use an app or wait for a routine appointment to assess them.
Sources and limits
The linked adult stroke rehabilitation guideline is written for its own jurisdiction and health system. It is a source for discussion, not a promise of local services or eligibility.

Sources and review for this draft
Clinical review: not recorded · Patient/carer review: not recorded · Translation review: English original · Review date: not recorded
Treatment and Outcomes of Cervical Artery Dissection in Adults: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association (2024)
Source record checked 2026-10-12. A scientific statement is not a personalised treatment plan. Verify exact passages before publishing individual claims.
ESO guideline for the management of extracranial and intracranial artery dissection (2021)
Source record checked 2026-10-12. Do not generalise recommendations across anatomical groups or haemorrhagic and ischaemic presentations.
Information needs of people who have suffered a stroke or TIA and their preferred approaches of receiving health information: A scoping review (2025)
Source record checked 2026-10-12. The review concerns stroke/TIA, not cervical artery dissection specifically; it searched only German- and English-language literature through June 2023 and did not assess the quality of included studies.
Information provision for stroke survivors and their carers (2021)
Source record checked 2026-10-12. The review found uncertain or small effects for several outcomes, particularly for carers; it does not demonstrate that this app improves health outcomes.
Stroke rehabilitation in adults: recommendations (2023)
Source record checked 2026-10-12. Adult stroke rehabilitation; do not apply all recommendations to dissection without stroke. UK service entitlements are country-specific.
