Prepare for a follow-up appointment
Choose what matters most, make room for questions and leave with a clear next step to discuss. About 2 min read.
Draft information · review pending

Read guide aloud
Read one section at a time
Before the appointment
•
Pick one or two questions that matter most if you have limited time or energy.
•
Write down unfamiliar terms or results you want explained.
•
Decide whether you want someone to come and what you want them to do.
•
Ask the clinic whether there is anything you should bring; this app does not collect or interpret medical records.
•
If reading or remembering is difficult, ask whether someone can provide information in another format.
During the appointment
•
Ask what is known, what remains uncertain and what happens next.
•
Ask whether any follow-up imaging is planned for you and, if so, what its purpose is.
•
Ask what you need to know about medicines prescribed or changed for you, and who to ask if you have a concern.
•
Ask who to contact with a non-emergency question and how to reach them.
•
Ask for a written summary or accessible format if that would help.
•
You can pause, take a break or ask the clinician to explain something another way.
Before you leave
•
Check that you understand the next step and who is responsible for it.
•
Ask when or how you should expect to hear about pending results.
•
Ask what to do if you cannot reach the listed contact with a routine question.
•
Decide what you want the person who came with you to remember or share.
After
Use the instructions from your own care team. If something is unclear, contact the service they identified. This checklist cannot supply missing clinical advice or determine whether a situation is urgent.

Sources and review for this draft

Treatment and Outcomes of Cervical Artery Dissection in Adults: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association (2024)
Source record checked 2026-10-12. A scientific statement is not a personalised treatment plan. Verify exact passages before publishing individual claims.
ESO guideline for the management of extracranial and intracranial artery dissection (2021)
Source record checked 2026-10-12. Do not generalise recommendations across anatomical groups or haemorrhagic and ischaemic presentations.
Information needs of people who have suffered a stroke or TIA and their preferred approaches of receiving health information: A scoping review (2025)
Source record checked 2026-10-12. The review concerns stroke/TIA, not cervical artery dissection specifically; it searched only German- and English-language literature through June 2023 and did not assess the quality of included studies.
Information provision for stroke survivors and their carers (2021)
Source record checked 2026-10-12. The review found uncertain or small effects for several outcomes, particularly for carers; it does not demonstrate that this app improves health outcomes.
Stroke rehabilitation in adults: recommendations (2023)
Source record checked 2026-10-12. Adult stroke rehabilitation; do not apply all recommendations to dissection without stroke. UK service entitlements are country-specific.
