CAD
In this app, CAD means carotid artery dissection.
The abbreviation can mean other things elsewhere. Check how it is used in your own report.
Source: Treatment and Outcomes of Cervical Artery Dissection in Adults: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association ↗
Dissection
A problem involving the wall of an artery. A cervical artery dissection affects an artery in the neck, such as a carotid or vertebral artery.
This general definition cannot explain the location, cause, seriousness or care plan in an individual case.
Source: Treatment and Outcomes of Cervical Artery Dissection in Adults: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association ↗
Extracranial / intracranial
Extracranial means outside the skull; intracranial means inside the skull.
These words describe location. They do not explain what a finding means for a person.
Source: Anatomy, Head and Neck, Skull ↗
Infarct
An area of tissue injury associated with loss of its blood supply.
The word alone does not tell you the location, size, timing, cause or effects of a finding.
Source: NICE guideline glossary: infarct ↗
Occlusion
A blockage or closure of a vessel.
Ask the clinician who reviewed the test what vessel and area the report describes and what it means in your case.
Source: Carotid Artery Stenosis: Diagnosis and Treatment ↗
Pseudoaneurysm
A pocket of blood outside the usual layers of an artery wall, connected to the artery through a defect in the wall.
A definition cannot establish the location or significance of a finding in an individual report.
Source: Pseudoaneurysm ↗
Stenosis
Narrowing.
The word alone does not say how much a vessel is narrowed or what that means for you.
Source: Definition: stenosis ↗
VAD
Vertebral artery dissection.
A clinician can explain which artery and part of the artery are involved in an individual case.
Source: Treatment and Outcomes of Cervical Artery Dissection in Adults: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association ↗
