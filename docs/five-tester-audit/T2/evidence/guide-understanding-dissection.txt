Understanding cervical artery dissection
A plain-language starting point for carotid and vertebral artery dissection. About 2 min read.

What the words mean
A cervical artery dissection is a problem in the wall of an artery in the neck. The carotid arteries and vertebral arteries carry blood toward the brain. A dissection can affect either type of artery; the location and effects vary from person to person.
VAD means vertebral artery dissection. In this app, CAD means carotid artery dissection. The carotid and vertebral arteries in the neck carry blood toward the brain. A clinician can explain which artery is involved in an individual case.
Why experiences differ
A dissection may be found during assessment for local symptoms, may be associated with a stroke, or may be discovered in another way. Imaging findings and symptoms do not tell the whole story about an individual’s outlook. Only the treating team can interpret a person’s tests and care plan.
Questions you could take to your team
•
Which artery and part of the artery are involved?
•
What did the imaging show, and what remains uncertain?
•
What follow-up is planned, and whom should I contact with a non-emergency question?
•
Which information is specific to my situation and which is general?
Sources and limits
This draft is informed by the linked AHA scientific statement, ESO guideline and patient-information sources. Their recommendations and populations are not interchangeable. This draft has not been clinically reviewed and cannot explain an individual scan or recommend treatment.
