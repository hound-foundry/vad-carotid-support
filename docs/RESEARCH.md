# Research seed list

The source catalogue at [content/references.json](../content/references.json) holds bibliographic metadata, links, intended use, evidence limits, audience and review status in a format we can later use in the app.

## How to read its status

Every entry begins as a candidate. Finding a bibliographic record does not mean we read the full paper, assessed its quality, verified every claim or received permission to republish text. The 2017 survivor book was identified through its ISBN and public bibliographic listing; only its description is visible in this research pass. It remains copyrighted. The app may link to it, but must not copy, scan, excerpt, translate or redistribute it without appropriate rights.

A source’s publication year matters: current clinical claims need checking against later guidance and studies. Distinguish adult extracranial cervical dissection from intracranial or major-trauma presentations when evidence does. Distinguish findings for people who had stroke from findings for people who did not.

## First editorial priorities

1. Establish a current clinical overview and a plain-language definition, anchored to current professional guidance.
2. Create a dedicated pathway for dissection without stroke and explain what available evidence does and does not say.
3. Prepare sourced, non-prescriptive appointment questions about treatment choices and follow-up.
4. Review longer-term quality-of-life, fatigue and emotional wellbeing evidence without presenting exploratory results as prevalence estimates.
5. Mark lifestyle and return-to-activity advice according to evidence strength; direct individual clearance back to the treating team.
6. Add local stroke emergency information only after country-specific review.

Sources were collected on 12 October 2026. Recheck every source before writing or publishing clinical advice.
