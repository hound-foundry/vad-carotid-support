# Product brief

## Purpose

Help people affected by vertebral artery dissection (VAD) or carotid artery dissection (CAD), their families and carers, and interested readers find understandable, sourced information and practical support. The project is informed by lived experience and is free, open source and public benefit. No formal charity status, clinical endorsement or healthcare service is claimed.

## People and entry points

Let people begin from what they need today rather than a presumed clinical pathway:

- “I have a dissection, without a stroke.”
- “A dissection caused a stroke.”
- “I’m supporting someone.”
- “I’m here to learn.”

These are navigation choices, not a diagnostic or eligibility questionnaire. Someone can change paths without losing their place.

## Practical value

- Short explanations of the arteries, what “dissection” means, common investigations and unfamiliar clinical words.
- Clearly labelled evidence links and dated updates; explain uncertainty and differences between studies.
- Separate resources for dissection without stroke, stroke-related care and recovery, and everyday questions relevant to either group.
- Appointment preparation: editable questions, a user-controlled export to show a clinician, and a “How to support me” sheet the person chooses what to share.
- Carer guidance for listening, practical support, appointments, communication difficulties and fatigue.
- Space for emotional wellbeing, uncertainty, persistent symptoms and recovery that is not visible to other people.
- Curated links identifying publisher, audience, language, evidence type, publication date and last checked date.

## Clinical boundaries

The app refers personal diagnosis, test interpretation, medication choices, dose changes, prognosis and activity clearance back to the user’s own clinician. It does not calculate an individual’s stroke or recurrence risk; screen for VAD/CAD; triage symptoms; recommend treatment; or promise recovery. A disclaimer alone does not determine medical-device status, so intended purpose and each feature require documented regulatory assessment.

New, sudden symptoms potentially consistent with stroke require urgent local emergency action. Do not route a possible emergency through appointment booking, notes, search, a chat or a routine doctor-contact suggestion. Show emergency numbers and national help links only after local verification.

## Privacy and languages

Initial design: no accounts, adverts, donations, analytics, behavioural tracking or network sync. Saved resources, questions and notes stay on the user’s device; this remains a design target until implemented and tested. Explain device backup behaviour and give users clear export and deletion controls. Never silently upload notes, include them in analytics/crash reports, or place sensitive data in share links.

Language choice is independent of country. Planned languages: English, Russian, Ukrainian, Lithuanian, Latvian, Estonian, Spanish, French, Japanese, Simplified Chinese and Traditional Chinese. Translate original short guides and interface strings only. Link to outside articles in their source language; do not claim an external article is translated. Review each translation, with special review for emergency and clinical information.

## Accessibility and human-centred design

Design for people who may be tired, distressed, have vision or balance symptoms, use assistive technology, have cognitive or communication effects after stroke, or read in a second language. Keep navigation predictable, pages short, focus visible, tap targets large, text resizable, contrast strong and motion optional. Provide clear headings, plain language, screen-reader names and exportable text. Test with people with lived experience before release; do not ask them to disclose a diagnosis or medical records.

## Success criteria

People can find the right explanation, tell sourced information from expert opinion and lived experience, prepare for a care-team conversation and manage their private information without an account. Patients without stroke and their carers can recognise themselves in the app. Do not claim clinical effectiveness or outcome improvement without appropriate evidence.
