# Patient, carer and clinical specialist audit

Audit date: 13 October 2026  
Revision reviewed: `f565aa9` plus the card date/field corrections in this working revision  
Scope: static Expo web preview, source content, local data tools, review metadata and release gates

## Patient and carer view

### What is useful now

- The home page offers routes for basics, stroke-related recovery, no diagnosed stroke, carers and general learning.
- The “What do you need right now?” panel gives direct paths to glossary, appointments, support preferences, cards and urgent help.
- Guides support one-section reading, larger text, read-aloud, printing and appointment follow-up.
- Appointment and support sheets let a person choose only a few items, preview them, save locally, export explicitly and delete them.
- Personal and milestone cards are separate, local-first tools with visible limits and explicit export actions.
- Urgent help is persistent and explicitly tells people not to wait for the app.

### Friction and severity

| Severity | Finding | Required disposition |
|---|---|---|
| High | A tired or post-stroke user still encounters many cards and controls before a single next action. | Keep the quick-start panel prominent; validate with timed synthetic tasks. |
| High | Card fields can contain medicines and contacts, so a shared or printed card can expose sensitive information. | Keep preview, “entered by me” wording, explicit export and delete; test with synthetic values. |
| High | Text scale is applied through browser zoom; high-contrast, theme and spacing preferences need manual verification across all components. | Accessibility review remains a release gate. |
| Medium | Milestone sharing may create social pressure or reveal health history. | Keep neutral wording, optional survivor label, no direct posting and explicit social-copy warning. |
| Medium | External resources and Facebook candidates may change or require identity disclosure. | Keep verification dates and external-service warnings visible. |

## Clinical specialist view

### Strengths

- The app does not interpret scans, calculate risk, recommend treatment, change medicines or clear activity.
- Guides distinguish general information from an individual care plan and repeatedly refer questions back to the treating team.
- Stroke-related research is labelled as broader stroke evidence where appropriate.
- Spoon theory is labelled as a metaphor rather than a clinical measure.
- Emergency wording tells people to contact local emergency services and not wait for an appointment.
- Content, glossary, prompts, card warnings and emergency copy are represented in the claim ledger.

### Clinical stop-ship issues

- No qualified clinician has approved the current wording.
- Source records are candidates and do not prove that every claim has been appraised.
- Emergency language still requires explicit clinical and local-emergency review.
- Medicine, imaging, prognosis and follow-up wording must be checked against the exact current sources and limits.
- Translation can change the meaning of clinical or emergency text and requires separate review.

## Privacy and technical view

- Automated tests cover malformed storage, bounded fields, explicit save/delete, card separation and no-network/no-log checks for existing tools.
- The review register correctly remains blocked pending human review.
- Browser storage is not encrypted by this app and exported/printed/social copies are outside deletion control.
- Hosting/CDN logs and real-device backup behavior still require deployment-specific review.

## Release decision

The preview is suitable for continued development and controlled synthetic testing. It is not yet suitable for claiming clinically reviewed patient guidance. The release remains blocked until the six tracks in `docs/RELEASE_DECISION.md` have dated evidence and named reviewers with permission.
