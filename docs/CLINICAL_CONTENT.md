# Clinical content and review

## Current status

All content in this starter repository is unapproved for patient use. The source catalogue is an editorial starting list, not a systematic review. No listed paper or book has been fully appraised by a clinical reviewer through this project. The 2017 patient book was identified from bibliographic listings; no chapter content was inspected or copied.

## Editorial workflow

For every guide, record: audience, exact claim, source URL/DOI and publication date, source type, relevant page/section, what the evidence can and cannot show, author/editor, clinical reviewer and qualifications verified for this task, review date, language reviewer where applicable, licence/permission basis, country applicability, next review date and revision history.

Keep four things distinct: what a study reports; what a guideline recommends; an expert consensus opinion; and a person’s lived experience. Show uncertainty, population, follow-up period and relevant study limits beside numerical claims. Never turn a group estimate into an individual prediction. Do not copy article passages or book chapters when a link and original explanation will work.

A clinical claim is not release-ready until the source has been checked, an appropriately qualified clinical reviewer has confirmed the wording and limitations, and the review is recorded. Emergency guidance, medication content, activity restrictions, imaging follow-up and prognosis require explicit clinical review. Translations require a competent language reviewer; high-risk clinical text also needs clinical review in the target language.

Patient and carer accounts are voluntary, informed, revocable contributions. Obtain explicit permission for the exact content, named attribution choice, translations, storage and distribution. A person’s permission does not license a book, article or another person’s story.

Use the separate [patient and carer content review guide](USABILITY_REVIEW.md) for usability feedback. A usability review can identify unclear or unhelpful wording, but it cannot approve a clinical claim or replace qualified clinical review.

## Useful literature starting points

See the structured [reference catalogue](../content/references.json). Starting sources include the 2024 American Heart Association scientific statement, the 2021 European Stroke Organisation guideline, the 2024 patient quality-of-life study, the 2026 exploratory mental-health follow-up, research on antithrombotic treatment, the 2021 sports expert review and NICE adult stroke rehabilitation recommendations.

The [clinical review packet](CLINICAL_REVIEW_PACKET.md) maps the current draft pages to their review questions. It is preparation only; no reviewer or approval is implied.

The evidence base is uneven. Research into diagnosis and acute management does not necessarily answer common questions about life after discharge, people with no stroke, fatigue, anxiety, family support, work, travel or returning to activities. These gaps should be visible, not filled by confident-sounding guesses.
