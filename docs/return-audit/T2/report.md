# T2 first-use assessment

**Mode:** First-use persona assessment from parent-actuated captures and action records. I did not operate the browser; route choices and captured actions are evidence supplied by the parent. I judged them independently against T2’s declared priorities. No reports from other testers or app source were consulted.

**Build:** frozen revision `8c4087e9fa2764ae3b5cd791c0d5a66834372936`, web bundle SHA-256 `f992acd985b2018f0dd2e23aa0a938496e6134ea54df43e677a8471eaa89c67e`.

**Ratings:** Navigation 1/2; reading and access 1/2; practical usefulness 2/2; data and output trust 2/2; interruption recovery 1/2. **Total 7/10.** No confirmed critical failure, so no critical cap applied. Confidence is limited because every journey was actuated by the parent, captures show states rather than independent discovery, and there is no fresh human-use or completion-time evidence.

## Numbered journey

1. **Starting point and routes — shared evidence.** Home presents Guides, Prepare an appointment, Make a card, Help someone, and saved work with short explanations. The captured menu contains all nine destinations, and each route capture has a persistent Urgent help link. The shared route records also show home/back/forward/reload actions and a useful 404 page with Home and Menu. T2’s appointment entry itself was opened directly by the parent, so this does not show how quickly I would discover it. At 320 px with 200% in-app text, the menu remains keyboard reachable, but the capture shows crowded, partly clipped labels and requires scrolling.

2. **Guide, glossary, and return — shared evidence.** The guide library offers seven topics, search, audience filters, and a glossary link. Guide captures show section reading controls and visible draft/review status. A saved guide returned to section 2 after an in-app interruption with dark theme and roomier spacing; this is useful progress feedback, though a full browser reload was not checked for reading position. Searching the glossary for “MRA” returned no term. The page offers the tests-and-terms guide and an appointment prompt as next steps, but the missing definition may leave a first-time reader with extra work. Urgent Help clearly says to contact local emergency services and not wait for the app; it has no local number because country details are unverified. These are interface observations, not a clinical review.

3. **Appointment sheet and pacing — T2 evidence.** The opening screen has a clear title and a short “choose what matters most” instruction, but presents three sections and many checkboxes in one long page. “Optional viewing options” contains a one-section mode, section count, and Next/Previous controls. That makes progress more visible and better fits T2’s preference for one chunk at a time. The review and export controls remain well below the prompts, notes, and privacy copy, so reaching them requires substantial scrolling. The captured 390 px one-section view still places the preview below the section content.

4. **Appointment interruption and saved output — T2 evidence.** One selected question and a synthetic note stayed in the sheet after leaving for Home and returning. The preview showed the exact selected question and note, and clearly said changes were unsaved until Save. Explicit Save followed by reload restored both; Export sheet produced a text file containing those same items and the safety statement. Delete offered a confirmation; Escape canceled, and a later confirmed deletion left zero selections and no note after reload. A generic automation selector failed before the visible Escape action; that is an automation intervention, not an app fault. The export locator auto-scrolled to the button, so its capture does not establish easy user discovery.

5. **Reading mode and place reset after leaving — T2 evidence.** With one-section mode on, the recorded trace advanced to section 2, went Home, then reopened appointments. On return, one-section mode had reset to the all-sections view and the section 2 indicator was absent. This trace was captured after the earlier deletion, so it does not show whether question/note content returned. A separate earlier trace (`02-resumed-draft`) does show the unsaved selection and note returning after Home. For someone managing attention or memory difficulties, losing both the chosen reading mode and place can require remembering how to resume.

6. **Support and both cards — shared T4 evidence.** Support preferences offered selectable prompts, a note, a review sheet, explicit Save, reload restoration, Export, and delete confirmation. The medical/communication card and milestone card both had optional fields, a review step, an edit path, explicit save, and reload restoration. Their previews and saved data stayed separate. Output checks found the chosen text in each card’s text/PDF output and excluded the other card’s synthetic markers and hidden date. The milestone also had PNG output. The saved list names card types but does not show personal labels, keeping details off the list while requiring opening a card to identify its contents. Exported and printed copies are stated to remain outside app control. Card delete confirmation explains the effect; Escape leaves it open with Cancel focused, unlike the sheet’s Escape behavior.

7. **Display and keyboard — shared T3 evidence.** Text and display settings expose 100–200% app text, roomier spacing, high contrast, dark theme, reduced motion, and simplified reading. A keyboard path reached all nine menu destinations at 320 px and at 844×320 with 200% app text; focus remained visible in the captured states. The menu capture at 320 px is visually crowded and partly clipped while scrolling. This was app text scaling, not browser zoom. No claim is made about true browser zoom, NVDA, or audible speech.

## General health and next improvement

The strongest fit for T2 is the combination of short instructions, selectable prompts, preview-before-save, clear saved/unsaved status, and explicit local-data and export notices. Saved appointment and card data restored as expected in the supplied records. The biggest observed friction is that appointment reading returns to all-sections mode and loses section progress after Home and return. The next improvement should restore the last appointment section and one-section preference, or show a clear “you were on section 2 of 3” return cue.

Other concrete improvements are to reduce clipping and nested scrolling in the menu at 320 px/200% app text, and add common imaging abbreviations such as MRA to the glossary or provide a more direct explanation for an empty search. A consistent Escape response across delete confirmations would make cancellation behavior more predictable. These are usability recommendations from current evidence, not clinical approval.

**Not tested:** true browser zoom; NVDA or another screen reader; audible guide speech; OS print-dialog cancellation; native iOS/Android screens; malformed browser storage; and whether reading position survives a full browser reload. No timing or task-completion claim is made.
