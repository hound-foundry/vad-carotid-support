# Patient and carer content review

This guide supports patient and carer review of the project’s draft pages and tools. It is a product-usability activity, not clinical research, medical advice, or a substitute for qualified clinical review. No review is recorded as complete by creating this guide.

## Invite and review safely

- Invite adults to review a named page or tool. Participation is voluntary; a reviewer can skip any prompt or stop at any time.
- Explain that the material is a draft and is not approved for patient use. Do not ask reviewers to share a diagnosis, treatment, medical record, scan, hospital, clinician, or personal story.
- Ask reviewers to respond to the wording and task: what was clear, useful, confusing, missing, tiring, worrying, or hard to act on.
- Do not collect names, contact details, health history, or identifying quotations as part of the review notes. If a reviewer accidentally includes sensitive details, remove them from the working notes and do not publish them.
- Do not publish or quote a reviewer's words as a testimonial. Any separate use of an identifiable account requires a separate, specific permission process.
- The project must decide and explain a private, time-limited way to receive and delete review notes before arranging a session. Do not put sensitive feedback into public GitHub issues or pull requests.

## Prompts for a page review

Show one short page at a time, then ask:

1. What question did you expect this page to help with?
2. Which part was clear or useful? Which part was hard to understand?
3. Did anything feel alarming, dismissive, overly reassuring, or like a prediction about you?
4. Could you tell what was general information, what the sources can show, and what needs a conversation with your own clinician?
5. What would you want to do or ask after reading it? Was that next step clear?
6. Was the amount and layout manageable if you were tired, distracted, using a small screen, or using assistive technology?

For carer-facing pages, also ask whether the wording respects the person's choices and privacy, whether it gives carers practical help without asking them to take over, and whether it acknowledges the carer's own support needs.

For the appointment and support-preferences sheets, check whether the choices make sense, whether any important choice is missing, whether the preview matches the person's selections, and whether save, export, and delete behavior is understandable. For saved guides, check whether people understand that topics are stored on the current device, can reveal interests to others with device access, and can be exported or deleted. Use synthetic entries only during software testing.

## Record and resolve feedback

Record only the guide or tool, section, review date, reviewer role if they choose to share it, the usability issue, and the change made or reason no change was made. Keep notes brief and free of personal health information. Report themes without identifying a reviewer.

After revising a page, offer the revised wording to reviewers for a short confirmation when practical. Record usability review as approved only after the intended audience has reviewed the material and the resulting changes have been checked. This status is separate from clinical approval, source appraisal, translation review, and legal review. Never change a guide to `published` merely because it passed patient/carer review.

## Review checklist

- [ ] The reviewer knew the page was an unapproved draft.
- [ ] No medical records or personal health details were requested or retained.
- [ ] The page's audience and purpose were clear.
- [ ] Wording, length, and next steps were understandable and manageable.
- [ ] The page respected patient choice and carer boundaries.
- [ ] Feedback was considered and revisions were made or documented.
- [ ] Clinical and other release gates remain accurately recorded.
