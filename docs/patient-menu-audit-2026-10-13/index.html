Patient and carer menu audit — before repairs

The repairs have since been implemented and checked. This page preserves the original findings. Read the repair report and after screenshots.

13 October 2026 · inspected app revision 8ce542e · no app source changed

The home and card choices are useful. The inner experience still demands too much reading and remembering. Three confirmed functional failures need repair before describing the experience as suitable for low vision or memory difficulties.

What already helps

  • Four clear home actions, readable text and calm rounded surfaces.
  • A communication statement created a useful card preview without a name or medical details.
  • The practical card draft remained available in app memory; the saved hub labelled it as unsaved.
  • Fatigue search returned the intended guide, without putting the query in the address.
  • Urgent help remained easy to find and led to direct generic instructions.
  • Escape closed the menu and returned focus to Menu. Keyboard Enter on the skip link focused the active main region.

Findings and repairs

P1 marks a confirmed access or draft-retention failure. P2 marks an important usability or semantic issue. These are engineering priorities, not medical risk scores.

P1 · Enlarged menu cannot reach its bottom

At 390 × 844 and 200% app text, Saved occupied y=827–936 and About y=940–995. Pointer scrolling over the menu left scrollTop at zero. The main region collapsed to zero height while the expanded menu was outside its scroll area.

Why it matters: Someone needing large text cannot reach the saved-items and privacy menu entries using ordinary scrolling.

Repair: Give the menu a bounded, independently scrollable region while keeping Close menu and urgent help reachable. Test the open menu at small heights and large text, not just individual routes.

Evidence: step 25, step 26.

P1 · Appointment and support drafts disappear on return

An invented appointment note was entered, then Home → Prepare an appointment returned an empty note with no warning. A support note and checked statement also disappeared after Home → Menu → Support preferences. These were unsaved drafts; saved records were not deleted.

Why it matters: The person must reconstruct their wording and selections, and may not remember what was lost. The card tool behaves differently because it preserves drafts.

Repair: Retain both sheet drafts in app-session memory, as cards do. Keep persistent Save explicit; do not introduce silent storage. Show saved/unsaved status near the working controls.

P1 · Simplified reading can strand the reader on one section

Global Simplified reading was enabled. On the recovery guide, turning off Read one section at a time unchecked the control and removed Next/Previous. Only the first of five sections remained; the other section headings were absent.

Why it matters: The control appears off, but the remaining information cannot be reached from that view.

Repair: Use one effective reading-mode state for the checkbox, visible sections and pagination. Either support a local override or clearly present the global setting as authoritative.

P2 · Useful content is buried below setup and repeated notices

The guide library, recovery guide and glossary showed no guide body/definition in the first phone viewport. The recovery page places save/print/read-aloud controls and three reading/action panels before its first section.

Why it matters: Opening a topic feels like another setup task. Tired readers must scroll before receiving the help they came for.

Repair: Keep a short visible draft label, then show useful content or one immediate action. Move detailed review/privacy explanations and secondary tools into labelled disclosures. Retain review dates, source limits and personal-care boundaries.

P2 · Returning to reading still requires remembering

After saving the recovery guide and reaching section 2, returning through Home → Saved → Saved guides → recovery reset the one-section mode and reading position. The library search also reset on return.

Why it matters: Bookmarks help find the topic but do not help the person continue where they stopped.

Repair: Preserve reading mode/section and search state in app-session memory. Offer a generic Continue reading entry without exposing health details on Home. Discuss explicit local persistence separately.

P2 · The carer entry gives a reading list before a practical action

Help someone starts with a draft notice and six guide links. The support-sheet and appointment actions come after that list.

Why it matters: A worried family member has to make another reading choice before doing something useful.

Repair: Lead with a few small actions: ask what help is wanted, prepare one question, or review support preferences together. Keep optional reading underneath.

app/supporting-someone.tsx:9

Evidence: step 15.

P2 · Guide text-size radios do not expose checked state

The rendered Standard text/Larger text radios had aria-selected true/false, but aria-checked was absent on both. This was a DOM check, not an NVDA test.

Why it matters: Assistive technology may not communicate the current choice correctly. The page-local controls also duplicate Text and display.

Repair: Use proper radio checked state and keyboard behavior, or consolidate these controls into the global display preferences.

app/guide/[slug].tsx:70; app/guide/[slug].tsx:71

Evidence: step 7.

P2 · MRI search leads to an unhelpful dead end

Searching the glossary for MRI returned zero terms, a try-a-shorter-word message and a clinician prompt. There was no route to the tests/scans guide from that empty state.

Why it matters: The app promises help with report words but cannot give a useful next destination for this query.

Repair: Link the empty state to Tests, scans and unfamiliar terms. Expand terms and synonyms only with the required content review.

app/glossary.tsx:14

Evidence: step 20.

P2 · Resource trust labels are confusing and overly technical

The directory says 5 verified resources while every item shows Source appraisal: candidate and Clinical review: not recorded. Rights annotations and internal source-type names are displayed on each item.

Why it matters: The reader cannot tell whether verified means the link was checked or the clinical content was approved.

Repair: Say Links checked when that is the evidence. Present audience, publisher, link date and relevant limits plainly; retain detailed rights/appraisal records behind Source details.

app/resources.tsx:13

Evidence: step 21.

P2 · The no-typing communication path is hidden after typing fields

The practical card starts with name and a large free-text box. Choose communication statements sits below them and is collapsed.

Why it matters: Someone with communication or typing difficulty can miss the easiest way to create a useful card.

Repair: Offer the selectable statements first, with optional own words and personal details underneath. Keep the two card models separate.

Scope and limits

Browser walkthrough of every menu destination, from the perspective of a tired patient or carer. A design/engineering audit, not a patient study or clinical review.

Codex in-app Chromium browser; desktop entry and 390 × 844 phone viewport; 100% and 200% app text. Forms used a separate localhost:4175 origin with invented notes. Existing personal saved data on localhost:4173 was not opened, changed or deleted.

No NVDA, VoiceOver, TalkBack, physical-phone touch, manual browser zoom, clinical accuracy, external-site validation or patient/carer session was performed. The scroll failure was reproduced with browser pointer scrolling. Card preview was exercised; downloads and printing were outside this audit. No app source was changed.

Code checks used the Web Interface Guidelines as a secondary reference. Observations and reproduction steps above come from this browser walkthrough; they do not establish WCAG conformance.

Recommended repair order: preserve unfinished sheets, make the enlarged menu scrollable, correct simplified-reading state, then shorten the guide and carer entry flows.
